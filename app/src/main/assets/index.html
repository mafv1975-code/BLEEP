Before every session

  • Green: normal walking, no pain, legs recovered → planned session.
  • Amber: heavy legs after work, poor sleep, or soreness → halve the running repetitions / do one strength set, or rest. If your warm-up does not improve how you feel, rest.
  • Red: pain, limping, swelling, or unusual weakness → stop training. Persistent or recurring symptoms need a clinician or physiotherapist’s assessment. Chest pain, faintness or severe unusual breathlessness need urgent help.

Progress without overload

Advance only when the previous session felt controlled and you were back to normal the next day. Otherwise repeat the last comfortable session or reduce it.

Keep at least 48 hours between strength sessions. A running day immediately after strength is optional: skip it if sore. Put demanding sessions on a lighter walking day where possible; never stack missed sessions.

You are off 7–11 December: reduce optional long walks then. No hard strength work in the final week.

Leg exercise guide · controlled, comfortable movement
  1. Chair sit-to-stand: use a stable chair against a wall. Feet about hip-width, lean slightly forward, stand and lower slowly for about 2 seconds. Knees follow the toes. Use hands or a higher seat if needed. Main thigh exercise.
  2. Glute bridge: lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat. Gently lift your hips, pause briefly and lower. Avoid arching the lower back.
  3. Supported calf raise: hold a stable support, rise onto the balls of your feet and lower slowly.
  4. Optional low step-up from 24 November: only after two comfortable strength sessions, replace (do not add to) a sit-to-stand set with 5–6 step-ups per leg on a stable 10–15 cm step with a handrail. Lower slowly. Skip if painful or unsteady.

No weights, deep lunges or long wall sits are required. These sessions should leave you capable of doing a few more repetitions, not with exhausted thighs.

Treadmill and bleep-test guidance

Walking on the treadmill is for warm-up, interval recovery and cool-down, not additional mileage. On non-work days an optional 5–10 minute easy walk is enough if you want movement.

Use the safety clip. Start at 0% incline. Increase duration before speed; the suggested speeds are starting points, not targets. A treadmill cannot train the repeated braking and turning of a shuttle test.

The APK includes a simulated bleep track, but this edition uses your organiser’s recording for rehearsal to avoid claiming an unverified track is official. Official 15 m guidance and recording (internet needed).

Shorten the final steps into a turn, reach the required line and turn smoothly. Avoid an abrupt twist on a planted knee. Follow the organiser’s line and stopping rules.
